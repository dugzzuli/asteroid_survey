# Review outline

## Working scope

**Asteroid Research since 2001: Papers, Methods, Surveys and Resources**

The working period begins with the modern large-scale digital and inversion era. The final title and scope remain intentionally open until the literature pool is mature.

## Questions the review must answer

1. How have the data sources for asteroid research changed over the past 25 years?
2. How did discovery methods evolve from manual or rule-based procedures to automated pipelines?
3. Why is moving-object linking a persistent computational bottleneck?
4. How did large surveys change orbit research?
5. How did period measurement develop from dense light curves to sparse survey photometry?
6. What progress and limits define shape inversion since 2001?
7. How have asteroid phase-function models evolved?
8. How did WISE/NEOWISE alter diameter and albedo studies?
9. How did taxonomy move from spectroscopy to multi-band photometry and machine learning?
10. How have Gaia, ZTF, ATLAS, and related projects enabled population-level science?
11. Which problems has AI materially improved, and where do conventional methods remain stronger?
12. What bottlenecks will Rubin/LSST, CSST, and NEO Surveyor create or expose?
13. What are the major gaps in public datasets, software, and benchmarks?

## Candidate narrative

```text
Images → detection → linking → orbit → photometry → physical characterisation
```

## Decision gate

After 200–300 verified records and 30–50 priority-A papers, select one path:

- Large review: asteroid research in the era of astronomical surveys.
- Methods review: detection and characterisation in modern surveys.
- Data-driven review: automated discovery through physical characterisation.
