# Single-asteroid physical-characterisation study

## Research question first

Do not start from “we observed an asteroid”. Start from a missing, testable parameter: an ambiguous period, unknown pole/shape, possible YORP acceleration, rotational colour variation, uncertain phase function, or missing thermal constraint.

## Escalation path

| Level | Minimum evidence | Result | Suitable claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dense lightcurve over a rotation | Synodic period and amplitude | New or resolved rotation period |
| 2 | Multiple apparitions and geometries | Sidereal period, pole and convex shape | First/revised spin-shape model |
| 3 | Long historical baseline | Constant-spin versus YORP comparison | YORP constraint or upper limit |
| 4 | Multiband and phase-angle coverage | Colours, phase function and surface variation | Taxonomic/surface constraints |
| 5 | Thermal, radar, occultation or spacecraft data | Joint physical model | Integrated physical characterisation |

## Minimum data record

For each target, record LCDB period status, DAMIT model status, archival photometry, WISE/NEOWISE coverage, Gaia observations, radar/occultation availability, time baseline, phase-angle range, next apparition, and the exact unresolved question.

## Interpretation limits

One dense night can support a synodic period and amplitude. It does not by itself justify a unique pole or three-dimensional shape. Disk-integrated lightcurve inversion normally constrains a convex shape; non-convex structure needs independent data or a clearly qualified model.

## Manuscript logic

```text
previous data establish A and B
        ↓
C remains unknown or inconsistent
        ↓
new observations plus public archives
        ↓
method and validation matched to the missing parameter
        ↓
result, uncertainty, and remaining degeneracy
```
